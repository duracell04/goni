# Anticipatory delegation preserves the authority boundary

Goni may continuously infer what work is likely to be needed next. The inference
may use current state, active objectives, similar prior cases, execution
trajectories, procedural memory, and domain knowledge.

The authority invariant is independent:

> Prediction can create a hypothesis and preparation. Prediction alone cannot
> create permission.

Repeated behavior increases evidence that a future action is likely or preferred.
It does not enlarge a mandate, corridor, capability, budget, or other delegated
authority.

For an anticipated task, Goni may perform policy-permitted, reversible
preparation ahead of an explicit request. Any external or consequential commit
still requires authority that exists independently of the prediction.

Canonical formulation:

- predict likely work,
- preserve competing hypotheses when uncertainty is material,
- prepare within reversible and policy-permitted boundaries,
- commit effects only through existing authority,
- learn from outcomes without silently widening authority.

A compact engineering formulation is: **speculate on cognition; gate side
effects**.
