# Principal-agent theory motivates bounded delegation and monitoring

Ross formalizes the principal's problem of delegating decision-making to an
agent. Jensen and Meckling analyze agency costs associated with delegated
control. Eisenhardt reviews agency theory around information, uncertainty,
monitoring, and risk. Holmström studies the role of observability in principal-
agent arrangements.

Goni's interpretation is narrower than a literal transfer of the theory:
machine agents need not possess human interests for delegation to create
specification, observability, verification, and residual-loss problems.

The supported design implication is institutional rather than psychological:
delegated machine action benefits from explicit decision rights, monitoring
artifacts, revocation, and bounded authority.
